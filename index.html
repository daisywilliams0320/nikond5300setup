Exposure

Matrix metering · ⅓ EV

Start with A mode for stationary scenes, S mode for moving subjects. Inspect highlights and shutter speed after test frames.

Focus

AF-S still / AF-C moving

Viewfinder single-point for eyes, buildings and details. Dynamic-area 9 or 21 points for tracking. Use AF-C with continuous high for sports.

Colour

Auto WB · Standard

Standard is a safe all-rounder. Change to Portrait for people or Landscape for scenery; avoid heavy Vivid on grass and skin.

Light & contrast

Active D-Lighting Auto

Useful for contrasty daylight JPEGs. Switch Off for consistent studio-style exposure, tripod night shots, or exposure matching.

Lens

VR On handheld

Use lens VR / Optical VR where available for handheld still subjects; consider Off on a stable tripod. VR does not freeze moving cars or golfers.

Important: With the 18–140mm zoom, maximum aperture changes from f/3.5 at 18mm to f/5.6 at 140mm. For night racing, f/5.6 at the long end may require very high ISO; an f/2.8 or faster lens is a substantial upgrade. D5300's top shutter speed is 1/4000s and continuous high is approximately 5 fps under suitable conditions.

Quick camera logic

A = Aperture priority

You set f-number. Camera picks shutter. Check it isn't too slow. Best for scenery, architecture, portraits and static cars.

S = Shutter priority

You set shutter speed. Camera picks aperture. Auto ISO helps maintain exposure. Best for golf swings, moving cars and panning.

M = Manual

You set shutter and aperture. Choose fixed ISO for consistent tripod/night scenes or Auto ISO for changing action light.

PHOTOGRAPHY FUNDAMENTALS

Understand light. Master clarity.

Learn what each control actually changes, simulate exposure, and take five sharpness rules into every shoot.

Exposure is not ISO

Exposure = captured light

Shutter time and aperture control how much light reaches the sensor. Longer shutter time or wider aperture captures more light, but can introduce motion blur or shallower depth of field.

ISO = brightness / sensitivity setting

ISO changes the brightness of the camera's rendered result, not the amount of light captured at the same aperture and shutter. Raising ISO often reveals more noise and reduces highlight headroom.

Exposure compensation (±EV)

Tells automatic exposure to aim brighter (+) or darker (−). In A mode with fixed ISO, this usually changes shutter speed; in M with fixed ISO it does not change the actual exposure settings.

Example: f/8 · 1/250s · ISO 100 versus ISO 800: the sensor receives the same light but the ISO 800 JPEG renders roughly three stops brighter. Change 1/250s to 1/30s instead and the sensor collects roughly eight times as much light (if nothing else changes).

Five rules for crystal-clear D5300 photos

01

Focus precisely

Place a single AF point on the nearest eye for portraits. Use AF-C and dynamic-area AF for golfers and moving cars.

02

Use enough shutter speed

At 140mm start around 1/250s for handheld static subjects; go much faster for moving action.

03

Keep ISO as low as practical

Use ISO 100 in good light, but raise it to avoid blur. A sharp ISO 1600 shot beats a blurry ISO 100 shot.

04

Choose a suitable aperture

f/5.6–f/8 is a strong general range. f/8 is excellent for scenery; open wider for portraits or dark conditions.

05

Use VR appropriately

VR helps with handheld camera shake on still subjects, not subject motion. Turn it off on a stable tripod when appropriate.

Actual D5300 menu-by-menu setup

Open menus with G (MENU). “P” / information edit screen controls quick settings. These are the D5300's actual option families; firmware and lens support can affect availability. Recommended values are a baseline, not Nikon factory defaults.

Picture Control tuning · JPEG-first

Picture Controls influence JPEG appearance and embedded previews. They are not substitutes for correct exposure or white balance. These are suggested custom starting adjustments, not factory Nikon profiles.

How to adjust

G → Shooting Menu (camera icon) → Set Picture Control → choose Standard / Portrait / Landscape / Neutral / Vivid → press right to modify → adjust Sharpening, Contrast, Brightness, Saturation and Hue where supported → OK (J). Start from the selected profile's defaults and change only the specified sliders.

For skin: prefer Portrait and gentle contrast. For golf: Standard or Landscape with modest saturation. For cars: Standard with slightly higher contrast, keeping highlights from clipping.

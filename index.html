Quick camera logic

A = Aperture priority

You set f-number. Camera picks shutter. Check it isn't too slow. Best for scenery, architecture, portraits and static cars.

S = Shutter priority

You set shutter speed. Camera picks aperture. Auto ISO helps maintain exposure. Best for golf swings, moving cars and panning.

M = Manual

You set shutter and aperture. Choose fixed ISO for consistent tripod/night scenes or Auto ISO for changing action light.

Three things to check before every shot

1 · Is it moving?

Yes: protect shutter speed. Walking people 1/250s; fast sports 1/1000s or faster.

2 · Is the face too dark?

Check face exposure. Against bright glass, use positive EV, spot meter carefully or add fill light. Outside may clip.

3 · Is it sharp?

Check focus on the eye, shutter speed and zoom level. Increase ISO when necessary instead of accepting blur.

PHOTOGRAPHY FUNDAMENTALS

Understand light. Master clarity.

Learn what each control actually changes, simulate exposure, and take five sharpness rules into every shoot.

Exposure is not ISO

Exposure = captured light

Shutter time and aperture control how much light reaches the sensor. Longer shutter time or wider aperture captures more light, but can introduce motion blur or shallower depth of field.

ISO = brightness / sensitivity setting

ISO changes the brightness of the camera's rendered result, not the amount of light captured at the same aperture and shutter. Raising ISO often reveals more noise and reduces highlight headroom.

Exposure compensation (±EV)

Tells automatic exposure to aim brighter (+) or darker (−). In A mode with fixed ISO, this usually changes shutter speed; in M with fixed ISO it does not change the actual exposure settings.

Example: f/8 · 1/250s · ISO 100 versus ISO 800: the sensor receives the same light but the ISO 800 JPEG renders roughly three stops brighter. Change 1/250s to 1/30s instead and the sensor collects roughly eight times as much light (if nothing else changes).

Five rules for crystal-clear D5300 photos

01

Focus precisely

Place a single AF point on the nearest eye for portraits. Use AF-C and dynamic-area AF for golfers and moving cars.

02

Use enough shutter speed

At 140mm start around 1/250s for handheld static subjects; go much faster for moving action.

03

Keep ISO as low as practical

Use ISO 100 in good light, but raise it to avoid blur. A sharp ISO 1600 shot beats a blurry ISO 100 shot.

04

Choose a suitable aperture

f/5.6–f/8 is a strong general range. f/8 is excellent for scenery; open wider for portraits or dark conditions.

05

Use VR appropriately

VR helps with handheld camera shake on still subjects, not subject motion. Turn it off on a stable tripod when appropriate.

One-time D5300 setup

JPEG Fine · Large

High-quality JPEG files for direct posting. Choose sRGB colour space. RAW+JPEG Fine is optional insurance if you later change your mind about editing.

Picture Control

Start with Standard: sharpening 5, contrast +1, saturation +1. Use Portrait for natural skin and milder contrast. Review actual colour before keeping a recipe.

White balance

Auto for changing daylight. For a series under the same light, try Daylight, Cloudy, or a custom preset to avoid colour shifts between frames.

Active D-Lighting

Auto can help JPEG shadows in contrasty daylight. It cannot recover completely blown highlights or make very dark faces perfectly exposed.

Auto ISO with limits

Use a maximum around ISO 1600 in daylight and 3200 indoors; raise it when a sharp moving subject matters more than noise. Always inspect shutter speed.

Compose for the post

Leave extra space for a 4:5 feed crop and 9:16 Stories. The D5300 captures 3:2 stills, so shoot with a little room around people and important details.

Choose your finished JPEG look

Golf greens & resort exteriors

Standard · sharpening 5 · contrast +1 · saturation +1 · Daylight or Auto WB. Watch white sand and skies for clipping; avoid oversaturated greens.

Portraits & guest photos

Portrait · sharpening 3–4 · contrast 0 · saturation 0 · Auto WB. Place focus on the near eye and check face brightness.

Hotel rooms & architecture

Standard or Neutral · mild contrast · Auto or preset WB. Keep verticals straight and inspect bright windows; reposition rather than relying on processing.

Ballroom & indoor events

Standard · Auto ISO up to 3200–6400 when necessary · check skin tones under stage LEDs. Use a fast enough shutter and avoid mixed-light colour casts.

Picture Control tuning · JPEG-first

Picture Controls influence JPEG appearance and embedded previews. They are not substitutes for correct exposure or white balance. These are suggested custom starting adjustments, not factory Nikon profiles.

How to adjust

G → Shooting Menu (camera icon) → Set Picture Control → choose Standard / Portrait / Landscape / Neutral / Vivid → press right to modify → adjust Sharpening, Contrast, Brightness, Saturation and Hue where supported → OK (J). Start from the selected profile's defaults and change only the specified sliders.

For skin: prefer Portrait and gentle contrast. For golf: Standard or Landscape with modest saturation. For cars: Standard with slightly higher contrast, keeping highlights from clipping.

Record your first video — step by step

  1. 1. Turn the camera on. Insert a formatted SD card and set the mode dial to P for an easy first attempt.
  2. 2. Find the Live View switch (Lv). It is the lever next to the mode dial on top of the D5300. Rotate it: the scene appears on the rear LCD.
  3. 3. Compose and focus. Point the camera at your subject. Half-press the shutter button to focus before recording.
  4. 4. Press the red-dot movie-record button. This is a small separate button near the shutter button on top of the camera — not the big photo shutter. Look for the red recording indicator on the LCD.
  5. 5. Press the red-dot button again to stop. Rotate the Live View switch to exit Live View.
  6. 6. Review your clip. Press the Playback (▶) button, select the movie, and press OK (J) to play.
First-time setup: MENU → Shooting Menu → Movie settings → Frame size/frame rate → choose 1920×1080 25p or 50p where available. Set Movie quality to High and Microphone to Auto sensitivity to start. Video uses the rear screen, not the optical viewfinder.

Which video settings should I use?

🎬 Resort cinematic B-roll

1080/25p · approximately 1/50s shutter where controllable · f/5.6–f/8 · lowest practical ISO · Auto WB or consistent preset.

Slow, stable camera moves; film golf course, lobby, rooms and pool. Use a tripod or stabilizer. In strong daylight, an ND filter may be needed to hold 1/50s.

🏌️ Golf swing / water splashes

1080/50p · around 1/100s for natural motion · widest useful aperture · ISO to suit light.

Good for 50%-speed playback on a 25p timeline. Focus on the golfer's swing area in advance if continuous AF hunts.

🎤 Ballroom speaker / interview

1080/25p · around 1/50s · wide aperture · Auto ISO as necessary · fixed WB under consistent lighting.

Tripod recommended. Prioritise clean audio using an external microphone or separate recorder. Check LED flicker.

🏎️ Racing car action

1080/50p · start near 1/100s for motion blur or faster for crisp individual frames · track smoothly.

Use the lens VR handheld, keep safely behind circuit barriers, and expect video AF to be slower than modern mirrorless cameras.

Important D5300 limitation: Manual video exposure control is more restricted than on modern video-focused cameras. The camera's Movie settings include Manual movie settings; to control shutter speed and ISO manually in M mode, enable this option and set the exposure before recording. Aperture should be chosen before entering Live View. Avoid assuming still-photo S mode gives full independent shutter control during filming. Video autofocus may hunt and its motor may be audible.

Video menu checklist

Resolution and frame rate

1920×1080 at 25p for everyday Malaysia footage; 50p for smoother action. Options can depend on video mode (PAL/NTSC).

Flicker reduction

For Malaysian 50 Hz lighting, choose 50 Hz in Setup Menu → Flicker reduction when appropriate. Try 25p/50p to minimise indoor flicker.

Focus

AF-F for continuous live-view autofocus, or AF-S / manual focus for controlled static clips. Manual focus is often smoother for cinematic work.

Sound

Check microphone level and monitor for wind, AC noise and lens focusing noise. The D5300 has a microphone input for compatible external mics.

Field operations & troubleshooting

V5 · Interactive masterclass

Focus / Focusing Academy

Focus determines which distance looks sharp. Aperture determines how much depth around that distance appears acceptably sharp. Focus and depth of field are different!

Place the AF point on the nearest visible eye for a still portrait. This illustration is conceptual, not an optical simulation.

AF-S · SingleAutofocus

For still people, food, architecture, stationary cars and group photos. Half-press to acquire and lock focus; recompose carefully if depth of field is shallow.

AF-C · ContinuousAutofocus

For people walking, golf swings and moving race cars. Keep the selected point over the subject while half-pressing; tracking continues as distance changes.

MF · ManualFocus

For fireworks and difficult low light. Switch lens to M, magnify Live View on a distant bright target, focus carefully, then avoid touching the ring.

Single-point vs Dynamic 9/21/39

Single-point: you choose exactly where to focus. Dynamic-area: you choose the initial point, with neighboring points helping track moving subjects. 9 for predictable motion; 21/39 when movement is harder to follow.

3D-tracking vs Auto-area

3D-tracking attempts to follow your selected subject across points; Auto-area chooses the subject for you. Auto-area is convenient but may focus on the wrong person.

Viewfinder vs Live View

The optical viewfinder uses the 39-point AF system. Live View uses different AF-area options: Face-priority, Wide-area, Normal-area and Subject-tracking. Do not confuse the two menus.

Field practice · 10 minutes

Photograph a stationary person with AF-S Single-point on the eye. Ask them to walk toward you and change to AF-C Dynamic 9. Finally, photograph a distant illuminated building in Live View with manual focus. Zoom in on playback to compare accuracy.

V5 · Visual composition studio

Composition / Professional Composition Academy

Composition is how you direct attention using placement, lines, perspective, light, colour and empty space. Choose the subject first, then remove distractions.

Practice: place the horizon on the upper or lower third, then deliberately try a centered symmetrical frame. Compare which communicates your subject more clearly.

Rule of thirds · Rule of thirds

Put a golfer or feature near an intersection. Position the horizon on a third when sky or ground deserves emphasis.

Symmetry · Symmetry

Stand exactly in the center of a lobby, corridor or entrance. Check equal spacing on both sides.

Leading lines · Leading lines

Use fairways, roads and corridors to guide attention to the main subject.

Framing · Framing

Shoot through doors, arches, windows or trees. Keep the surrounding frame from overwhelming the subject.

Negative space · Negative space

Leave clean sky, wall or turf beside the subject for premium marketing headlines.

Directional space · Directional space

Give racing cars and walking guests space in the direction they travel.

Layering · Foreground, middle ground, background

Use foreground bunker, middle-distance fairway and distant hotel towers to add depth.

Visual balance · Visual balance

Balance a large subject with smaller objects, contrasting colours or light areas.

Clean edges · Frame edges

Before shooting, scan every corner for cropped limbs, rubbish, signs or distracting objects.

Perspective & focal length · Perspective

18mm emphasizes nearby space; 70–140mm narrows the angle of view. Camera position controls perspective.

Horizon & verticals · Horizon

Keep horizons level and buildings upright unless intentionally using dramatic diagonals.

Light as composition · Light and shadow

Use brighter areas to draw attention to faces, trophies or a car, while allowing less important areas to recede.

At your resort, shoot the same golf hole in five ways: thirds, centered, leading lines, foreground layering and negative space for poster text. Keep the subject consistent so you can compare the visual storytelling.
